PREVENTIVE CARE

A practical pre-ride motorcycle checklist

A short walk-around before a ride can help you notice visible problems before you enter traffic. This checklist is a general reminder, not a substitute for the maintenance schedule or inspection procedure in your motorcycle owner's manual.

Taporaa Vehicle Care · General educational guide

1. Tyres and wheels

Look for visible cuts, bulges, embedded objects, unusual wear or a tyre that appears underinflated. Check tyre pressure when the tyres are cold using the pressure specified by the vehicle manufacturer, not a random value from another motorcycle. If a tyre is damaged or loses pressure repeatedly, do not ride until it has been inspected.

2. Brakes

Check the front and rear brake levers or pedal for normal feel and adequate free movement as described in the manual. Look for visible fluid leaks or obvious damage around brake components. If braking feels weak, spongy, unusually hard or inconsistent, do not ride; arrange an inspection.

3. Lights, indicators and horn

Check the headlamp, tail lamp, brake light, turn indicators and horn. Make sure mirrors are secure and positioned so you can see behind you. Replace or repair failed safety equipment before riding.

4. Controls and visible components

Check that the throttle and handlebars move normally and that cables, switches and foot controls appear secure. Look for loose parts, damaged wiring or anything rubbing against a wheel or hot component. Do not ride if steering or control movement is restricted.

5. Chain and drive system

For motorcycles with a chain, look for obvious damage, excessive slack, rust or unusual noise. Check and adjust lubrication and slack only according to the manufacturer's instructions and only if you know the correct procedure. Keep hands and clothing away from moving parts.

6. Fluids, fuel and documents

Look for fresh oil or other fluid leaks. Check engine oil and other service items using the method and intervals in the manual. Confirm sufficient fuel and carry the documents and protective equipment required for your journey.

Quick checklist

  • Tyres: condition, pressure and visible damage.
  • Brakes: normal feel and no visible leaks.
  • Lights: headlamp, tail/brake lamp and indicators.
  • Controls: throttle, steering, mirrors and horn.
  • Chain/drive: obvious damage or abnormal slack.
  • Fluids: no fresh leaks; check levels per the manual.

Frequently asked questions

How often should tyre pressure be checked?

Follow the schedule in your owner's manual and check more often if you notice pressure loss or a change in handling. Use the manufacturer's specified pressure and check when tyres are cold.

Can I ignore a small oil leak?

No. A leak can indicate a fault and may create a road or fire hazard depending on where the fluid goes. Have it inspected before continuing to ride.

Does this checklist replace regular servicing?

No. Routine servicing and component inspections must follow the model-specific maintenance schedule.

Safety note: This article is general information only. Vehicle designs and manufacturer instructions differ. Stop if the vehicle is unsafe to operate and contact a qualified service professional. Do not attempt work beyond your training or the equipment available to you.
