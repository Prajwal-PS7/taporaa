ROAD-SIDE SAFETY

What to do when your motorcycle gets a flat tyre

A sudden loss of tyre pressure can affect steering, braking and stability. The safest response is to slow down smoothly, move away from traffic and avoid riding farther than necessary on a damaged or deflated tyre.

Taporaa Vehicle Care · General educational guide

1. Move to a safer place

Keep both hands on the handlebar, avoid abrupt steering or braking, and reduce speed gradually. Move to a safe area away from moving traffic if you can do so without creating more danger. Switch off the engine and make yourself visible to other road users. Do not stop in a live lane to inspect the tyre.

2. Do not continue riding on a flat tyre

Riding on a deflated tyre can damage the tyre and wheel and may make the motorcycle difficult to control. Do not try to reach a workshop by riding slowly on a visibly flat or damaged tyre. If the vehicle is in an unsafe location, prioritise your personal safety and request suitable roadside or recovery assistance.

3. Observe the damage without taking risks

From a safe position, note whether the tyre appears deflated, whether an object is visible, and whether the wheel or tyre has obvious damage. Do not remove a nail or other embedded object at the roadside unless a qualified person has advised you to do so. A repair may not be safe or possible depending on the damage, tyre construction and manufacturer guidance.

4. Share useful details when requesting help

Tell the provider whether the puncture is on the front or rear wheel, your vehicle make and model, your exact pickup location or a clear landmark, and whether the vehicle is safely parked. Ask whether an on-site inspection is possible or whether the motorcycle needs to be transported to a workshop.

5. Confirm the work and price first

Ask whether the provider recommends a repair or replacement and why. Confirm the total estimate, including inspection, labour, parts, valve or tube replacement if applicable, and any travel or towing charges. After the work, ask the provider to check inflation pressure against the vehicle or tyre manufacturer's specification.

Quick checklist

  • Do not ride on a visibly flat or damaged tyre.
  • Stop in a safe place away from traffic.
  • Share wheel position, vehicle model and location.
  • Ask whether on-site repair is appropriate.
  • Confirm the estimate before authorising work.

Frequently asked questions

Can every punctured tyre be repaired?

No. Repair suitability depends on the location and size of the damage, tyre type, internal condition and manufacturer guidance. A provider should inspect it before recommending repair.

Should I remove the nail or screw?

Avoid removing an embedded object at the roadside. Removing it can cause a rapid air loss and may make later assessment harder. Ask a tyre professional to inspect it.

Can I ride slowly to a nearby shop?

Do not ride on a visibly flat or damaged tyre. Arrange suitable assistance or recovery instead.

Safety note: This article is general information only. Vehicle designs and manufacturer instructions differ. Stop if the vehicle is unsafe to operate and contact a qualified service professional. Do not attempt work beyond your training or the equipment available to you.
